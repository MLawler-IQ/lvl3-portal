# World-Class Keyword Analysis for Local Home Services SEO at Scale

## TL;DR

- A world-class keyword analysis for local home services is not a keyword list; it is a decision system that ties every query to a business outcome (booked call, local pack visibility, organic ranking, or AI citation) and survives the fact that in 2025-2026 rank and volume no longer predict traffic. The single biggest differentiator between world-class and mediocre is deriving CTR and opportunity from the client’s own GSC data plus grid-based local visibility, not trusting tool defaults (volume, keyword difficulty, generic CTR curves).
- Standard national keyword practice breaks down for local in specific, nameable ways: proximity makes single-point rank tracking misleading (a business can be #1 at its front door and invisible three miles away), the local pack and organic results for the same query are two different algorithms rewarding different signals, and monthly-average volume actively lies for extreme-seasonality trades like HVAC (per Stacker’s February 2026 analysis using Ahrefs Keywords Explorer, “AC repair” searches increase 266% in July while “furnace repair” dominates January with a 137% increase; heating system repair swings roughly 594% peak-to-valley).
- Recent breaks that invalidate old methods: Google’s removal of the &num=100 parameter (September 2025) broke third-party rank data below the top ~10-20 and caused an artificial GSC impression collapse (87.7% of sites lost impressions per LOCOMOTIVE Agency analysis of 319 properties); AI Overviews cut organic clicks materially (Pew: users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared vs 15% without; Ahrefs: up to a 58% position-1 CTR drop by December 2025); and GEO/AEO citation tracking is mostly vendor marketing with no established, automatable method yet.

## Key Findings

**1. Prioritize by decision-changing impact, not tutorial order.** The components that most change what an agency should do, roughly in order: (a) business-value mapping to service lines and job economics; (b) local visibility measurement via grid/SoLV rather than single-point rank; (c) realistic CTR/opportunity sizing from the client’s own data adjusted for SERP features and AI Overviews; (d) intent classification including local-service-specific intents (emergency, repair-vs-replace, maintenance plan); (e) SERP-overlap-validated clustering mapped to page types; (f) seasonality shaping; (g) cannibalization detection; (h) competitive/SoV and aggregator reality; (i) seed/universe construction; and only then (j) keyword difficulty and raw volume, which are the most overrated inputs.

**2. World-class vs mediocre with identical data.** The better analyst derives a site-specific CTR curve from GSC and discounts it for AI Overview presence; treats tool volume and KD as ordinal hints, not cardinal truth; validates clusters against live SERPs instead of trusting semantic similarity; tracks local performance as Share of Local Voice across a grid; maps every cluster to a page type and a dollar value; and builds a prioritization score that is transparent and reversible rather than a black-box composite. The mediocre analyst sorts by volume, sorts by KD, exports, and calls it a strategy.

**3. What to stop doing.** Keyword density and LSI keywords are dead. Exact-match volume fetishism is invalidated by Google Keyword Planner’s bucketing and near-variant aggregation. Keyword difficulty as gospel is wrong, especially for local (link-based KD misreads local intent). Single-keyword-per-page dogma is obsolete. Ignoring zero-volume keywords is a real error for local. Monthly-average volume is dangerous for HVAC. “Long tail = easy” is only sometimes true.

## Details

### The frame: what “world-class” actually means here

A world-class analysis is a repeatable pipeline whose output is a prioritized action plan where each line item names: the target query cluster, the page that should own it, the realistic opportunity (clicks or calls, not impressions), the business value, and the confidence level. Every component below is judged against one question: does it change a decision? If it cannot be tied to organic rankings, local pack visibility, AI-answer citations, or booked calls, it is either cut or explicitly flagged as vanity.

The 2025-2026 context forces a philosophical shift that must be baked into the pipeline: rankings no longer predict traffic. Per SparkToro’s 2024 Zero-Click Search Study (Rand Fishkin, using the Datos clickstream panel), “In 2024, 59.7% of European Union Google searches and 58.5% of American Google searches resulted in zero clicks,” with only about 360 of every 1,000 US searches reaching the open web, and that was before AI Overviews scaled. The entire classical funnel of “volume x CTR by rank = traffic” is now a starting estimate that must be discounted, not an answer.

### Component: Business-value mapping (highest decision-changing impact, most-skipped)

**What it is:** Tying each keyword cluster to a service line, average job value, margin, close rate, demand seasonality, and capacity constraints. **Question it answers:** Which rankings are worth having? **Why it matters most:** In home services the spread in value between query types is enormous. “AC repair near me” (emergency, high close rate, immediate) is worth vastly more per lead than “how does a heat pump work” (informational, low intent). A furnace replacement is a four-to-five-figure job; a drain snake is not. **Data required:** Client’s own job data (average ticket by service line, close rate by lead source), which the agency usually must extract from the client’s field service software (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro) or CRM. This is data the user does NOT have in the listed stack and must request from the client. Cheapest sufficient source: a one-time export of jobs by service line with revenue and a rough close-rate estimate from the owner.

**How it is done / where methods differ:** The rigorous approach builds a value-per-lead table by service line, then multiplies estimated booked calls (not clicks) by value and margin. The lazy approach uses CPC as a value proxy. CPC is a defensible ordinal proxy when job data is unavailable (high CPC correlates with commercial value because advertisers bid it up), but it systematically understates emergency intent value and overstates broad informational terms. **Failure mode:** Optimizing for a high-volume, high-ranking term that produces tire-kicker leads the client cannot profitably serve, or driving emergency demand a capacity-constrained plumber cannot fulfill (a real constraint: a single-truck operator cannot take unlimited same-day calls, so ranking harder for emergency terms can destroy customer satisfaction and waste spend).

This component persists poorly in tools because no keyword tool has the client’s job economics. It is the clearest example of where human/client input beats any platform.

### Component: Local visibility measurement (grid / Share of Local Voice)

**What it is:** Measuring rank from many geographic points across a service area rather than a single location, and summarizing as Share of Local Voice (SoLV = percent of grid points where the business appears in the local 3-pack). **Question it answers:** Where do real customers actually see this business, and where is it invisible? **Why it matters:** Google’s local results are proximity-weighted. A business can rank first for searchers within a mile and tenth for searchers across town on the identical keyword. A single rank number (“you rank #3 for plumber in Chicago”) is therefore not just imprecise, it is misleading. **Data required:** A geo-grid rank tracker (Local Falcon, Places Scout, BrightLocal). Local Falcon popularized the geo-grid format in 2018 and charges per map pin scanned.

**Method variants and rigor:** Grid size (5x5 to 13x13+) and spacing (0.3 to 1+ mile) are the two knobs. Denser grids cost more scans but reveal block-level reality; rural areas need wider spacing. SoLV is the headline KPI that replaces average rank. **Cost:** Per-pin API/scan costs; a 250-location portfolio scanning weekly across multiple keywords is a material recurring expense, which forces sampling (below). **Failure mode:** Scanning a grid larger than the business can actually serve (inflating apparent weakness), or reporting a single blended “average rank” that hides the geographic shape entirely. The build-it-yourself option (grid math plus a SERP API) exists but the honest caveat is that cheap SERP APIs do not perfectly replicate Google’s proximity handling.

**Where national practice breaks:** This entire component does not exist in national SEO. National rank tracking assumes one SERP per keyword; local has effectively one SERP per searcher location. Any pipeline that tracks local clients with single-point rank tracking is doing it wrong.

### Component: Local pack vs organic are two different algorithms

**What it is:** Recognizing that for the same local query, the map pack and the organic results below it are ranked by different systems rewarding different signals. **Why it matters:** You cannot fix pack visibility with on-page content and you cannot fix organic with GBP optimization. Per Google Business Profile Help (“Tips to improve your local ranking on Google”), “Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity,” and the page then defines the third factor under the heading “Prominence,” noting it is “based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have.”

The most authoritative recurring evidence is the Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors survey (2026 edition published November 6, 2025; 47 experts scoring 187 factors), which quantifies the divergence:

- **Local Pack top factors (with scores):** Primary GBP Category (227), Proximity of Address to Point of Search (225), Keywords in GBP Business Title (223), Physical Address in City of Search (213), Business Open at Time of Search (189), high numerical Google ratings (181).
- **Local Organic top factors (with scores):** Dedicated Page for Each Service (210), Geographic keyword relevance of content (190), Quality/authority of inbound links (187), keywords in landing page title tag (179).
- **The divergence quantified:** Primary GBP Category is #1 in the pack but #85 in organic. “Dedicated Page for Each Service” is #1 organic but #24 in the pack. Proximity is #2 in pack but #41 in organic.

Category-group weightings for the pack (published by Whitespark only as a chart image, so treat as approximate): GBP signals ~32%, review signals ~20% (up from ~16% in 2023), on-page ~15%, behavioral ~9%, links ~8%, citations ~6-7%. The frequently-repeated “proximity = ~55% of ranking” figure could not be verified in Whitespark’s primary text and should be treated as an unverified secondary claim.

**Implication for keyword analysis:** Every local cluster must be tagged with whether it triggers a local pack (check by searching from a mobile device in the service area; if a map pack with pins appears, Google has classified it as local intent). Pack-triggering queries route effort to GBP/reviews/proximity; non-pack local queries route to service+city pages and links. Treating them as one bucket is a core mediocrity marker.

### Component: “Near me” / implicit-local vs geo-modified queries

Sterling Sky’s 2025 study (8,186 businesses across 200 cities, including plumber/HVAC/locksmith/garage-door/lawn-care “near me” queries, published November 2025 with Places Scout) produced findings that directly reshape keyword work:

- Adding “near me/near you” text to a website lifts localized ORGANIC rankings but “didn’t move the needle at all for local pack rankings.” So “near me” keyword optimization is a web/organic tactic only.
- There was a negative correlation between hiding your address and ranking in the local pack. A real home-services client that hid its address saw rankings “freefall” and recover when re-added. This contradicts Google’s own service-area-business guidance and matters for service-area vs storefront decisions.
- Review velocity/recency beats total review count: “the number of reviews you’ve gotten this month matters more than your total number of reviews.”

**Practical taxonomy for the pipeline:** Treat “hvac repair” (implicit local, resolves to searcher location), “hvac repair phoenix” (geo-modified, targetable with city pages), and “hvac repair near me” (implicit local, proximity + open-now heavy) as three distinct entities with different targeting even though tools may group them.

### Component: Realistic CTR modeling and opportunity sizing

**What it is:** Converting rank + volume into expected clicks (then calls) using a CTR curve calibrated to the client, adjusted for SERP features and AI Overviews. **Question it answers:** How much traffic/revenue is this ranking actually worth? **Why it matters:** Generic CTR curves describe an average of many sites with different brands and SERP layouts; they are wrong for any specific site. **Data required:** GSC query-level export (clicks, impressions, position) over 3-6 months. The user HAS this.

**Method (defensible standard):** Export query-level GSC data, remove branded queries (they inflate CTR), round average position to the nearest integer, bucket rows by position, and compute click-weighted CTR per position bucket. That table is the site’s own expected-CTR curve. Compare actual vs expected CTR per page to find snippet problems (underperformers) versus content that overperforms. Segment curves by page type (service page vs blog) because they differ. **Cost:** Low; free data, a script or spreadsheet. **Failure modes:** (a) GSC “average position” is an impression-weighted average of a query that may flicker between page 1 and page 2, so a page “at position 9” may never actually appear on page 1; (b) GSC drops long-tail/anonymized queries for privacy, skewing the curve; (c) most critically, post-September-2025 the num=100 removal changed the position and impression baseline, so any CTR curve blending pre- and post-September data is corrupted.

**The AI Overview adjustment (mandatory now):** Position-1 CTR must be discounted where AI Overviews appear. The evidence base (all 2025):

- Pew Research (July 22, 2025 report; 900 US adults, 68,879 searches, March 2025 data): users who encountered an AI summary clicked a traditional search result in 8% of all visits; those who did not encounter an AI summary clicked nearly twice as often (15% of visits). Only 1% clicked citations inside the AI summary. About 18% of searches in the panel triggered an AI summary.
- Ahrefs: 34.5% drop in position-1 CTR with AI Overviews (April 2025, ~300k keywords); updated to a 58% position-1 CTR drop in December 2025.
- Seer Interactive (September 2025): organic CTR fell 61% (1.76% to 0.61%) for queries where AI Overviews appear, across 3,119 informational queries.
- Semrush/Datos: AI Overview prevalence rose from 6.49% of queries (January 2025) to a peak around 25% (July) settling near 15.69% (November 2025).

The practical rule: for informational clusters likely to trigger AI Overviews, discount modeled clicks heavily (a 30-60% haircut on position-1 CTR is defensible depending on vertical). Home services transactional/local queries trigger AI Overviews far less than informational health/finance queries, so the emergency and service+city clusters are relatively protected, which is itself a strategic argument for prioritizing them.

### Component: Search volume estimation and its limits

**What it is:** Estimating demand. **Why it is overrated:** Google Keyword Planner (the root source for most tools) rounds volumes into roughly 60-85 logarithmic buckets and aggregates near-variants, so “HDMI” and “High-Definition Multimedia Interface” can show identical volume, and non-advertisers see only broad ranges like “1K-10K.” Clickstream-based tools (Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz) cross-reference GKP with panel data to de-group, but panels are samples and disagree. Per Ahrefs’ 2022 study of 72,000 keywords, their estimates were “roughly accurate” (within 50% of GSC impressions) in 60% of cases versus 45% for GKP, and GKP overestimated 54.28% of the time. So volume is an ordinal comparison tool, not a cardinal truth.

**GSC impressions are not demand.** GSC impressions measure what your site was shown for (supply), not total market demand. Do not substitute one for the other.

**Seasonality is where averages actively lie for HVAC.** A 12-month average hides that, per Stacker’s February 2026 analysis using Ahrefs Keywords Explorer, “AC repair” searches increase 266% in July, “furnace repair” dominates January with a 137% increase, “heating system repair” swings roughly 594% peak-to-valley, and “emergency AC repair” rises nearly 400% from winter to late fall. (A separate PipelineOn claim that a single heat wave can increase “AC repair” searches by 400% within 24 hours illustrates the intraday volatility.) Method: use Google Trends to shape a seasonality curve per cluster, then plan content and capacity to lead the season (optimize AC terms in January-March, not June). **Data required:** Google Trends (free), Keywords Everywhere or Semrush for baseline volume. The user HAS these. **Failure mode:** Prioritizing a keyword by its average volume in the off-season and concluding it is low value, or launching content at peak when it is too late to rank.

### Component: Keyword difficulty and a better local proxy

**What it is:** A 0-100 estimate of ranking difficulty. **Why tools disagree:** Ahrefs KD is primarily referring-domain count of the top 10; Semrush uses a multi-factor model (referring domains, authority score, SERP features, region) and tends to score higher; Moz uses Page/Domain Authority of the top 10. The same keyword can read KD 23 in Ahrefs, 58 in Semrush, “medium” in Moz. **Why link-based KD misleads for local:** For local-pack and local-organic queries, backlinks are far less decisive than proximity, GBP signals, reviews, and city-relevant content (see Whitespark divergence above). A link-based KD score for “emergency plumber [city]” is close to noise because the businesses winning are not winning on links. **A better local difficulty proxy:** Composite of (a) how many of the top organic results are national aggregators vs real local businesses, (b) review count/velocity of the top local-pack incumbents, (c) proximity clustering of incumbents, and (d) presence of a dedicated service+city page among competitors. This is buildable from SERP scrapes plus GBP data. **Cost:** SERP API credits per query. **Failure mode:** Treating any single-number KD as a go/no-go gate; it should never be more than one weighted input. Note Semrush’s Personal Keyword Difficulty (PKD), which calibrates KD to a specific domain’s authority, is a step in the right direction but still link-centric.

### Component: Clustering methodology

**What it is:** Grouping queries that one page can satisfy. **Three methods:**

- **SERP-overlap clustering:** Group keywords whose top-10 results overlap. Standard threshold: 3-4 shared URLs in the top 10 (roughly 40% overlap). This is the most trustworthy for page mapping because it reflects what Google actually treats as equivalent, and it prevents cannibalization. Cost: a SERP fetch per keyword (API credits), which is the expensive part. Implementations use Jaccard similarity on the top-10 URL sets and community detection on a keyword graph.
- **Semantic/embedding clustering:** Convert keywords to vectors, group by cosine similarity (0.75-0.85 threshold typical). Fast, cheap, no SERP calls. Blind spot: it groups by meaning and misses intent divergence (e.g., “how to roast coffee” vs “buy roasted coffee” look similar but need different pages) and misses cases where semantically different terms return identical SERPs.
- **Lexical clustering:** Group by shared tokens. Crude, cheapest, high error rate.

**Defensible standard:** Draft with semantic clustering for speed and scale, then validate every important cluster against live SERPs before committing a page. Never consolidate or publish money pages on semantic similarity alone. **Failure modes:** Over-merging (mixing intents on one page), under-merging (near-identical queries on separate URLs causing cannibalization), and using volatile SERP data as if it were stable (re-run every 3-6 months). At 250+ locations, full SERP-overlap clustering per market is cost-prohibitive, forcing the representative-market sampling approach.

### Component: Intent classification (with local-service-specific intents)

Standard four intents (informational, navigational, commercial, transactional) are necessary but insufficient for home services. The pipeline should add service-specific intent tags that change page type and priority:

- **Emergency** (“emergency plumber open now,” “24 hour AC repair”) - highest value, transactional, pack-triggering, low AI Overview risk.
- **Repair vs replace** (“furnace repair” vs “furnace replacement cost”) - different job value and funnel stage.
- **Brand** (manufacturer or competitor names) - navigational/commercial.
- **Financing** (“HVAC financing bad credit”) - commercial, high intent, often underserved.
- **Maintenance plan** (“AC tune up,” “maintenance agreement”) - recurring-revenue intent.
- **Part-specific** (“capacitor replacement cost”) - mixed intent, often informational.

**Method:** Classify by inspecting the live SERP (featured snippets/PAA/AI Overview signal informational; local pack + ads signal transactional-local). This is more reliable than keyword-string rules. **Failure mode:** Serving informational content on a transactional page or vice versa, which fails the searcher and dilutes the page’s ranking signal.

### Component: Seed generation and keyword universe construction

Sources, roughly in order of signal quality for local home services: (a) GSC queries the site already gets impressions for (real, first-party, free) - the user HAS this; (b) GBP “searches that showed your business” (directional, monthly, ~6 months history, terms under 15 impressions hidden as “<15”) - useful but limited; (c) service taxonomy expansion (services x cities x intent modifiers, generated programmatically); (d) competitor-based gaps (Semrush/Ahrefs keyword gap against true SERP competitors); (e) autocomplete/PAA/related searches; (f) crawl-based (Sitebulb export of existing URLs and their targeted terms). **Failure mode:** Building the universe from tool databases alone and missing zero-volume long-tail service+city+intent combinations that never appear in GKP but convert.

### Component: Zero-volume and low-volume keywords (matters more for local)

Tools report zero volume because of bucketing, aggregation, and sampling gaps, not because demand is truly zero. For local this is acute: “emergency AC repair [small suburb]” may show zero volume in every tool yet represent real, high-intent, low-competition demand. Ahrefs’ honest counter-caveat: most low/zero-volume keywords really do get roughly the estimated (tiny) volume, and the wins usually come because the keyword is a long-tail variant of a popular topic,  not because zero secretly means high. **Practical rule:** Do not build a bespoke page per zero-volume geo term (doorway risk, thin content). Instead, cover them via a well-structured service+city page and internal linking, and let aggregate long-tail capture happen. **Failure mode:** Spinning up hundreds of near-duplicate zero-volume city pages, which triggers doorway/scaled-content problems (below).

### Component: Multi-location strategy at 250+ locations

**The doorway-page constraint is the central risk.** Google’s spam policy explicitly targets “multiple pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page” and scaled-content abuse (mass near-duplicate pages). Industry practice: location pages should be roughly 40-60% unique, location-specific content, and should only exist where the business has genuine presence/capacity. The test is whether the page would exist for a user, not just for a keyword.

**Templating vs bespoke:** Pure templating with city-name swapping is both a doorway risk and low-performing. The defensible middle path is a strong template scaffold populated with genuinely local content (local projects, neighborhood references, regional issues, real service constraints). **Sampling vs full coverage:** At 250+ locations, full keyword analysis and grid tracking per location is cost-prohibitive. The rigorous approach is representative-market sampling: cluster locations by market type (metro density, competition, seasonality zone), analyze deeply in representative markets, and template the strategy to the cohort while spot-checking. **Prioritization of markets:** Invest first where SoLV is low but demand and job value are high and the business has capacity. **Cannibalization at scale:** Location pages competing with each other for the same non-geo term is common; each cluster maps to exactly one URL.

### Component: Cannibalization detection

**Method:** Use GSC query-to-page data (ideally via the BigQuery bulk export to escape the 1,000-row UI limit) to find queries where multiple URLs receive impressions. Signals of true (harmful) cannibalization: fragmented impression share across URLs, unstable rankings with URLs swapping positions, both URLs stuck mid-pack, declining CTR at stable position. A useful severity metric is the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) of click concentration across competing URLs. **Not all multiple-ranking is bad:** Two URLs ranking for different intents behind the same phrase can be fine. **Fixes:** merge, 301 redirect, canonical, or differentiate intent. **Failure mode:** Reflexively redirecting/merging without confirming intent, destroying a page that was legitimately serving a distinct intent. **Data:** GSC (the user HAS it); tools (Sitechecker, SEOcluster) automate but the logic is replicable.

### Component: Competitive analysis and the aggregator reality

**True SERP competitors vs business competitors:** The businesses ranking for the target queries are often not the client’s real-world competitors. **The defining local home-services reality:** National lead-gen aggregators (Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor, Yelp) dominate organic results for many home-service queries with domain authority and budgets a local business cannot match. Their critical weakness: they cannot rank in the Google Maps/local pack. This dictates strategy: for pack-triggering queries, invest in GBP/reviews/proximity where aggregators cannot compete; for organic queries dominated by aggregators, the realistic opportunity is often lower than a KD score suggests, and Google Local Service Ads (pay-per-lead, exclusive leads, appearing above the pack) become the pragmatic paid complement. **Share of voice:** BrightEdge patented an SOV metric;  enterprise platforms (seoClarity, Conductor, BrightEdge, STAT) weight it heavily. For local, SoLV (grid-based) is the more honest SOV analog than keyword-set SOV. **Failure mode:** Sizing opportunity against a KD score while ignoring that the page-1 organic results are all aggregators the client will never outrank.

### Component: Conversion/booked-call attribution back to keywords

**The hard truth:** GSC gives query data but not conversions; GA4 does not provide keyword-level organic data (“not provided”); GBP gives call/direction counts but no downstream outcome and no post-2022 geographic breakdown of who requested directions. **Call tracking (CallRail, CallTrackingMetrics)** with dynamic number insertion is the standard bridge, attributing calls to source/landing page and, where available, keyword. But keyword-level call attribution is only as good as the referrer data, which is degrading. Best practice: push qualified calls (filtered by duration/quality via recordings) into GA4/Google Ads as conversions, and attribute at the landing-page/cluster level rather than pretending to have per-keyword call attribution. **GBP limitations to state plainly:** the “searches that showed your business” report hides terms under 15 impressions, is monthly with ~6-month history, commingles organic and paid, and website clicks “stop at the click” (add UTMs). The 2022 New Merchant Experience transition also changed “Views” to unique-user “Impressions,” so pre/post comparisons are unreliable. **This is where the honest answer is: full keyword-to-booked-call attribution is not reliably achievable; cluster-to-landing-page-to-call is.**

### Component: Content/page mapping and gap analysis

Each cluster maps to a page type: service page (core service, implicit-local), service+city page (geo-modified), blog/FAQ (informational, AI-Overview-exposed), or GBP-plus-reviews (pack-triggering). **New page vs optimize existing:** If a page already ranks page 1-2 for the cluster head term, optimize; if nothing ranks and the SERP shows a distinct page type winning, create. Validate with SERP-overlap clustering so you do not create a page that cannibalizes an existing one. **Failure mode:** Creating a new page for a cluster an existing page already half-owns.

### Component: Technical/crawl integration (Sitebulb)

Sitebulb crawl data intersects keyword analysis at: indexability of money pages (a service+city page that is not indexable earns nothing), internal linking depth to money pages (are the highest-value service pages more than 3 clicks deep or under-linked?), thin location pages (word count and uniqueness flags for doorway risk), and duplicate content across location pages (near-duplicate detection). **The user HAS Sitebulb.** **Failure mode:** A beautiful keyword strategy pointed at pages that are orphaned, thin, or duplicated. Crawl integration is what keeps the keyword plan grounded in what the site can actually rank.

### Component: Entity/topic modeling and schema

For local services, LocalBusiness/Service schema and consistent NAP across the site, GBP, and citations help Google (and increasingly LLMs) verify that a specific location performs a specific service in a specific market. This is a supporting signal, not a keyword-analysis core, but the keyword universe should inform which Service entities and service+area combinations get marked up. **Honest flag:** the direct ranking lift from schema is modest and often overstated by vendors; its clearer value is eligibility for rich results and machine-readability.

### Component: GEO / AEO / LLM visibility (flag: mostly vendor marketing)

**What is real:** AI Overviews and AI Mode are now in GSC. Google confirmed AI Mode counts toward GSC totals (June 17, 2025) and launched dedicated generative-AI performance reports (impressions only, no clicks; phased rollout starting June 3, 2026, beginning in the UK). So AI-surface impressions are becoming measurable in first-party data, though initially they are blended into Web Search totals with no separate AI Mode filter.

**What is mostly vendor marketing:** The proliferation of GEO/AEO citation-tracking tools (Profound, Peec, Scrunch, AEO Vision, and modules from Semrush/Ahrefs/Conductor/BrightEdge) claiming to measure and optimize LLM citations. The honest assessment: there is no established, validated, automatable method for driving local-service LLM citations, and academic work confirms LLM visibility is highly volatile (binary inclusion/exclusion, prompt-sensitive) and a “black box.” A candid practitioner summary from r/localseo: “Anyone who promises to rank you on ChatGPT is just selling air.” For a local home-services agency, LLM-citation tracking is not yet worth pipeline integration; the defensible move is to ensure strong conventional signals (which LLMs disproportionately draw from) and monitor AI-surface impressions in GSC. Flag any tool selling “AI rank tracking” as an established method: it is not.

**LLM referral traffic** can be measured crudely in GA4 by segmenting referrals from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini, etc., but volumes for local home services are currently negligible and the measurement is incomplete (much AI usage produces no referral).

### The September 2025 num=100 break (what it invalidates)

Google removed the &num=100 parameter around September 10-11, 2025. Consequences the pipeline must handle:

- Third-party rank trackers could no longer pull 100 results in one request; deep rank data (positions ~20-100) became expensive/unreliable. Treat any tool claiming precise deep rankings post-September 2025 with skepticism.
- GSC impressions collapsed artificially: 87.7% of sites lost impressions and 77.6% lost unique ranking keywords (analysis of 319 properties by Tyler Gargula, director of technical SEO at LOCOMOTIVE Agency, September 2025). Average position “improved” artificially as low-ranking bot-driven impressions vanished. The prevailing hypothesis is that much prior impression volume was synthetic (rank-tracker bots), so post-September data is arguably cleaner but not comparable to before.
- **Methodology requirement:** Mark September 2025 as a discontinuity in all GSC trend lines. Do not compute year-over-year impression or average-position deltas across it without adjustment. Rebuild CTR curves on post-September data only. Prefer GSC BigQuery bulk export for query-level analysis.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (foundation, weeks 1-4):** Wire GSC BigQuery bulk export for every client (escapes row limits, and is the clean post-num=100 data source). Build the site-specific CTR curve per client from post-September-2025 GSC data, segmented by page type, branded removed. Pull job-economics data from each client (value per service line, close rate) even if rough. These three moves alone move an agency from mediocre to credible.

**Stage 2 (local core, weeks 4-8):** Stand up grid-based tracking (Local Falcon or Places Scout) with SoLV as the headline KPI, not average rank. Tag every keyword cluster with pack-vs-organic (mobile SERP check) and intent (emergency/repair/replace/financing/maintenance/part). Route pack clusters to GBP/reviews, organic clusters to service and service+city pages.

**Stage 3 (universe and clustering, weeks 8-12):** Build the universe from GSC + GBP + service taxonomy + competitor gap. Cluster with semantic-first, SERP-validated for money pages. For the 250-location portfolios, define market cohorts and use representative-market sampling rather than full per-location analysis. Run cannibalization detection via GSC query-to-page with HHI severity.

**Stage 4 (prioritization and integration):** Build a transparent priority score: business value (job value x close rate x estimated booked calls) x realistic opportunity (CTR-curve-modeled, AI-Overview-discounted) x winnability (local difficulty proxy, aggregator presence) x seasonality timing. Keep every input inspectable and reversible; never ship a black-box composite. Integrate Sitebulb flags (indexability, thin/duplicate location pages, internal-link depth to money pages) as gating checks.

**Thresholds that would change these recommendations:** If DataForSEO is integrated, it becomes the cheapest scalable SERP-feature and SERP-overlap-clustering data source, enabling full clustering at portfolio scale (currently a cost gate). If AI Overview prevalence for a client’s transactional/local terms rises materially above the current low base, shift modeled-click discounts upward and reweight toward pack/booked-call KPIs. If a client’s LLM referral traffic in GA4 becomes non-trivial (say, consistently above 1-2% of organic sessions), stand up citation monitoring; until then, do not.

**On tooling honesty:** The user’s stack (GSC, GA4, GBP, Keywords Everywhere, Semrush, Sitebulb) covers everything needed except (a) job economics (get from client), (b) grid rank tracking (add Local Falcon/Places Scout), (c) scalable SERP data for clustering at 250+ scale (add DataForSEO). Call tracking (CallRail/CTM) is required for any real call attribution and is not in the current stack.

## Caveats

- **Whitespark category weightings** (GBP ~32%, reviews ~20%, etc.) are published only as a chart image; individual factor scores are from primary data and reliable, but the group percentages are approximate secondary citations. The “proximity = 55%” figure is unverified.
- **CTR-decline magnitudes vary widely by study** (15% to 89% depending on methodology, query type, and window) and most are informational-query studies; home-services transactional/local queries are less AI-Overview-exposed, so applying informational CTR haircuts to emergency/service clusters would overstate the damage. Google has publicly disputed the Pew methodology.
- **Many sources here are vendor blogs** (tool companies selling the capability they describe). Where a claim’s only source is a vendor selling that feature (GEO citation tracking especially), it is flagged as such and should not be treated as established method. Local Falcon’s “single-point tracking is useless” framing is itself from the grid-tracking vendor, though the underlying proximity point is well established across independent sources.
- **Seasonality percentages** (AC repair +266% July, etc.) trace specifically to Stacker’s February 2026 report using Ahrefs Keywords Explorer relative-interest data (syndicated via KVIA/KRDO), not absolute volumes; use them to shape timing, not to size absolute demand. The “+400% in 24 hours” figure is a separate PipelineOn claim.
- **What would be foolish to automate:** the new-page-vs-optimize decision on money pages (requires SERP judgment), the doorway-page line (requires judgment about genuine local value), intent disambiguation on ambiguous terms, and any LLM-citation “optimization.” These should stay human-in-the-loop.
- **The unsolved hard problems where good practitioners still disagree:** true search-volume accuracy (no source is authoritative), keyword-to-booked-call attribution (achievable only at cluster/landing-page level), how to weight AI-surface visibility with no click data, and whether GEO has any repeatable method at all.
